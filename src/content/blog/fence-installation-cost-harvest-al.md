**TL;DR:** A 6-foot wood privacy fence in Harvest, AL typically costs $25 to $40 per linear foot installed, or roughly $4,500 to $7,200 for a 180-foot backyard run. Vinyl privacy fencing runs $35 to $55 per foot, chain link $15 to $25 per foot, and aluminum or steel ornamental fencing $30 to $50 per foot. Gates, tear-out of an old fence, and sloped or rocky lots add to the total.

If you're in a newer Harvest subdivision and the three fence quotes sitting on your kitchen counter are a few thousand dollars apart, the gap almost always comes down to three things: the material, whether your HOA dictates the style, and what's underneath your yard. Harvest is unincorporated Madison County, built out mostly in 2000s-2020s subdivisions with deed-restricted covenants, so a fence project here involves a couple of extra steps that a fence company in open county land wouldn't deal with.

## How much does fence installation cost in Harvest, AL?

Most homeowners in Harvest pay between $3,500 and $9,000 to fence a typical subdivision backyard, depending on material, fence height, and linear footage. The bulk of that range is driven by material choice and how much of your lot perimeter you're enclosing.

A standard quarter-acre to half-acre Harvest lot usually needs 150 to 250 linear feet of fence to close in a backyard, not the full property line. Corner lots and larger acreage parcels further out toward Hazel Green or New Market run higher simply because there's more fence to build. Labor typically makes up 35 to 45 percent of the total, with material and hardware covering the rest.

## What's the per-foot cost by material?

Material choice is the single biggest cost driver, and it also determines how the fence holds up through North Alabama's hot, humid summers. Here's what installed pricing typically looks like per linear foot:

| Material | Typical height | Installed cost per linear foot |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure-treated wood privacy | 6 ft | $25-$40 |
| Cedar privacy | 6 ft | $30-$48 |
| Vinyl privacy | 6 ft | $35-$55 |
| Chain link | 4-5 ft | $15-$25 |
| Aluminum or steel ornamental | 4-5 ft | $30-$50 |

Wood privacy fencing remains the most requested option in Harvest because it meets most HOA covenants and costs less upfront than vinyl. The tradeoff is maintenance: pressure-treated pine needs staining or sealing every 2 to 3 years to hold up against Alabama's humidity and UV exposure. Vinyl costs more installed but needs essentially no maintenance beyond an occasional wash, which is why it's gained ground in newer subdivisions where homeowners want a 15-20 year fence they don't have to think about.

Chain link is the budget option but many Harvest HOAs restrict or prohibit it in backyards visible from the street, so check your covenants before you price it out.

## Do I need a permit or HOA approval to build a fence in Harvest?

Harvest is unincorporated, so fence permitting runs through Madison County rather than the City of Madison or City of Huntsville. Requirements vary by fence height and lot location, so confirm the current threshold with Madison County's permitting office before you schedule installation, since a corner lot or a fence over a certain height commonly triggers a permit even when a standard backyard fence under 6 feet in a side or rear yard does not.

HOA approval is the step most homeowners forget. The large majority of Harvest subdivisions have an architectural review process that requires approval before a fence goes up, and that approval often dictates material, height, and stain color, not just whether a fence is allowed at all. Submit your fence plan to the HOA before you order materials. A rejected submission after the posts are already in the ground is an expensive mistake.

One more call matters just as much as the HOA: Alabama 811, the statewide call-before-you-dig line. It's free, and Alabama law requires the call at least a few business days before digging post holes so utility companies can mark buried lines. Skipping it risks hitting a gas, water, or fiber line, which turns a simple fence job into a repair bill.

## What else should I budget for (gates, tear-out, and extras)?

Gates, old-fence removal, and difficult terrain typically add 10 to 25 percent on top of the base per-foot pricing. A single walk gate usually runs $200 to $500 installed, while a double drive gate wide enough for a mower or trailer runs $600 to $1,500 depending on material and hardware.

Tear-out of an existing fence adds a flat fee in most quotes, commonly $3 to $8 per linear foot for removal and disposal, more if the old posts are set in concrete. Sloped yards, rock encountered during post-hole digging, or a line that has to navigate mature trees also push labor time and cost up. Ask any fence contractor to walk your property line with you before quoting so these line items show up in the estimate instead of as a change order mid-project.
