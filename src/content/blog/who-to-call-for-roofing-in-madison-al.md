## How Do You Know If You Need a Repair or a Full Replacement?

A roof needs full replacement when the damage is spread across multiple roof planes or the shingles have lost granules broadly, not in one isolated spot. A roof needs only a repair when the damage is localized: a handful of cracked shingles around a vent boot, a small area of wind-lifted tabs, or a single leak traced to flashing.

Asphalt shingle roofs in North Alabama typically last 20 to 30 years depending on the shingle line, but hot, humid summers and spring hail accelerate wear well before that. If your roof is under 10 years old and the damage is contained to one slope, a repair is usually the honest answer. If the roof is already past 15 to 20 years and the damage is widespread, replacement is the more cost-effective path because patch repairs on an aging roof rarely hold a consistent warranty.

A contractor who recommends full replacement on a 3-year-old roof with one damaged section, without climbing up to actually inspect it, is a red flag. ## What Should You Ask Before Hiring a Roofing Contractor?

Ask for a written, itemized scope of work before paying any deposit, and ask how the contractor handles the manufacturer's warranty on materials versus their own labor warranty. Those are two different documents, and a contractor should be able to explain both without hesitating.

Other questions worth asking directly:

- Will you pull a city of Madison building permit for this job, or is it small enough to skip one?
- What shingle or metal product line are you installing, and what's the manufacturer's wind rating?
- Who is actually on the crew doing the work, your employees or a subcontractor?
- What happens if you find decking damage once the old roof comes off?

The [International Code Council](https://www.iccsafe.org) publishes the model building code that most Alabama municipalities, including Madison, adopt for roofing wind-load and fastening requirements. A contractor who references code requirements without being asked is usually one who actually pulls permits regularly.

## What Happens After You Call?

After you call, expect a scheduled on-site inspection first, not a same-day high-pressure sales pitch. A contractor walks the roof, documents the damage with photos, and gives you a written estimate that separates materials, labor, and any decking repair contingency.

If the damage is storm-related, bring your insurance policy number to that first visit.
