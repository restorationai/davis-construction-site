## Who do you call first for bathroom water damage?

Call a water damage restoration company first, before a plumber and well before a remodeling contractor. Restoration crews are set up to respond to active moisture, not scheduled plumbing work, and they bring the equipment to measure and dry a wet wall or subfloor properly.

A plumber can shut off and fix the leaking supply line, drain, or shower pan, and that's a necessary step. But a plumber isn't equipped to dry out a saturated subfloor or check whether water has tracked into the wall cavity behind the tub surround. That's a different skill set, with different equipment: moisture meters, air movers, and dehumidifiers sized to the space.

## What does the mitigation and dry-out process actually involve?

Mitigation starts with stopping the water source and extracting whatever is standing, then mapping how far the moisture has traveled before any drying equipment goes in. A technician uses a moisture meter on the drywall, baseboards, and subfloor around the leak to find the real boundary of the damage, which is almost always wider than the visible stain.

From there the typical sequence looks like this:

1. **Source control.** The leak or overflow is stopped, usually in coordination with a plumber if the source is a supply line, drain, or shower pan.
2. **Extraction.** Any standing water on the floor is pulled out with a wet vac or extraction unit.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Meters check drywall, baseboard, subfloor, and sometimes the ceiling below, to set a drying plan.
4. **Controlled drying.** Air movers and a dehumidifier run in the space, typically for 2 to 4 days depending on how much material got wet and what it's made of.
5. **Daily monitoring.** Moisture readings are tracked until the materials return to a normal, dry range, not just until things look dry.

Most bathroom leaks caught early, a slow supply line drip, a shower pan seepage found within a day or two, dry out completely with no demolition needed. That's the outcome to hope for, and it's the most common one when the leak is caught before it's been running for weeks.

## Will homeowners insurance cover a shower or bathroom leak?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a supply line that bursts, but typically exclude damage from a leak that ran slowly for months without being noticed or fixed. The distinction insurers draw is between a sudden event and gradual neglect.

A failed shower pan that's been seeping for a year and rotted the subfloor underneath is the kind of claim an adjuster will scrutinize closely, sometimes denying it as a maintenance issue rather than a covered loss. A supply line that blew out overnight is a much cleaner claim. Either way, document the damage with photos before any drying work starts, and keep the moisture-mapping report from the restoration crew. ## When does a bathroom actually need a remodel instead of just drying out?

A full rebuild becomes necessary when the subfloor has rotted, the drywall has delaminated, or mold has established itself inside the wall cavity, not just because water touched a surface. Drying alone fixes the vast majority of leaks caught within the first day or two.

Signs that point toward a rebuild rather than a dry-out: a subfloor that feels spongy or visibly sags underfoot, tile that's come loose because the substrate underneath failed, or a musty smell that persists after drying equipment has run its course. Mold can begin colonizing wet drywall and framing in as little as 24 to 48 hours in Alabama's humidity, which is why a fast response matters even when the leak looks minor. If mold has already set in, that work needs to be handled through [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/) before any rebuild framing goes back in, following guidance from the [EPA's mold resource](https://www.epa.gov/mold).
