## What should the best fence company in Harvest, AL have before you sign a contract?

A fence contractor worth hiring in Harvest should be licensed and insured, should give you a written estimate broken down by linear foot and gate, and should ask about your property lines before quoting a final number. Those three things weed out most of the bad bids on their own.

Ask directly: "Are you licensed and insured, and can I see proof?" A contractor who hesitates on that question is telling you something. You can also verify a residential contractor's standing yourself through the [Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board](https://hblb.alabama.gov/) for larger exterior projects, since home improvement work above certain dollar thresholds in Alabama falls under that board's oversight.

A written estimate should name the material, the post spacing, the gate hardware, and whether old fence removal and haul-off are included. If a quote is a single lump number with no line items, you have no way to compare it against another bid honestly. ## Which fence materials make sense for a Harvest, AL yard?

Wood privacy fencing and vinyl are the two materials most homeowners in Harvest end up choosing between, and the right pick depends on how much upkeep you want versus how much you want to spend up front. Pressure-treated pine privacy fence costs less installed but needs staining or sealing every few years in North Alabama's humidity. Vinyl costs more up front but won't rot, warp, or need repainting.

Chain link still shows up on larger rural-feeling lots or for side-yard and dog-run sections where privacy isn't the goal, usually at a lower per-foot cost than either wood or vinyl. A fence company that only pushes one material regardless of your situation isn't necessarily looking out for your budget. ## Do I need a permit or HOA approval to build a fence in Harvest?

Fence permits for Harvest properties go through Madison County rather than the City of Madison, since Harvest is unincorporated. Requirements and fee schedules change, so confirm current rules with the county before work starts rather than relying on what a neighbor did a few years back.

Most newer Harvest subdivisions carry HOA covenants that regulate fence height, material, and sometimes even stain color, and that approval usually needs to happen before the county permit is pulled. A fence company that's worked in Harvest's subdivisions before should ask about your HOA unprompted, not wait for you to bring it up.

Before any digging starts, your property lines should be confirmed, either from an existing survey or a fresh one, since a fence set even a foot over a line can turn into a dispute with a neighbor later. ## How do I compare fence quotes without getting burned?

Compare fence quotes line by line, not just the bottom number, and ask every bidder to put the warranty terms in writing before you sign anything. A quote that's thousands less than the others is usually missing something, whether that's post depth, gate hardware, or removal of the old fence.

A real warranty names what's covered (typically workmanship on posts and panels) and for how long, separate from the manufacturer's material warranty on the lumber or vinyl itself. If a bidder won't put warranty terms on paper, treat that as a red flag regardless of how good the verbal pitch sounds.

Timeline matters too. A straightforward 150-200 linear foot residential fence usually runs a few days once permits and HOA approval are in hand, not weeks, assuming materials are in stock. Get that timeline in writing along with the price.
