## What should you compare between pergola builders?

Start with the build type, because it changes the price, the maintenance, and who should even be building it. A cedar or pressure-treated wood pergola is the most common and most affordable option in this area, but it needs periodic staining or sealing in North Alabama's humidity. Aluminum pergolas cost more up front and need almost no maintenance. Louvered aluminum pergolas, with rotating slats that open and close, add real shade control for Harvest's long, hot summers but run higher still and require a builder who's actually installed the motorized or manual louver hardware before, not just framed a roof.

If you're adding a pergola over an existing patio or a new deck, ask whether the builder treats it as one integrated structure or two separate quotes. A pergola tied into new deck framing needs coordinated footings and load paths; bolting one onto an existing deck later is a different (and sometimes cheaper) job. ## What should a pergola estimate actually include?

A real estimate names the lumber or aluminum product line, the footing depth, the fastening hardware, and a warranty, in writing, before you sign anything. "We'll figure it out when we start" is not an estimate, it's a placeholder.

Ask every builder for these in writing:
- The specific material and product line (not just "wood" or "aluminum")
- Footing depth and whether it accounts for Harvest's clay-heavy soil
- Who pulls the permit, and who pays the fee
- A written warranty period on labor, separate from the manufacturer's material warranty
- A payment schedule tied to milestones, not a large deposit up front

A builder who hesitates to put any of this on paper is telling you something.

## Do Harvest pergolas need a permit and wind-load design?

Most pergola builds in Harvest fall under Madison County's permitting process, not the City of Madison, since Harvest is unincorporated. Many Harvest subdivisions also carry HOA architectural review requirements on top of the county permit, so check your covenants before you commit to a design. Confirm current footing and attachment requirements with Madison County before you finalize plans, since specifics can change.

North Alabama's spring storms and afternoon summer wind gusts mean a pergola needs real engineering, not just four posts and a roof. Structures built to recognized building code provisions, the kind referenced by the [International Code Council](https://www.iccsafe.org/), account for wind uplift on post connections and rafter spans, which is exactly where a poorly built pergola fails first. If a builder can't explain how their post-to-footing connection resists wind uplift, keep asking questions.
