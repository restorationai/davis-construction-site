**TL;DR:** A full roof replacement cost in Harvest, AL depends mainly on roof size (measured in squares), pitch and complexity, material choice, and whether the decking needs replacing. Architectural asphalt shingles are the most common choice and the lowest cost per square; standing-seam metal runs significantly higher but lasts longer. Insurance may cover a reroof after a documented hail or wind event, but age-related wear is on the homeowner. Most jurisdictions in Madison County require a building permit for a full replacement.

If you're in Harvest comparing three roofing bids right now and they're thousands of dollars apart, you're not imagining it. Roof pricing swings wildly based on a handful of variables that have nothing to do with how good or bad a contractor is: the size and pitch of your roof, whether your decking is sound, what material you choose, and whether your claim is tied to an insurance payout. Understanding those variables is the fastest way to tell whether a bid is high because of scope, or high because of markup.

## What drives the cost of a roof replacement in Harvest, AL?

The biggest factors are roof size, pitch, material, and the condition of the wood decking underneath the shingles. A 2,000 square foot single-story ranch with a simple gable roof costs far less to reroof than a two-story home with multiple valleys, dormers, and a steep pitch, even at the same square footage.

Roofers price in "squares," a roofing square being 100 square feet of roof surface. A typical Harvest-area home runs somewhere between 20 and 35 squares depending on footprint and roof style. Steep pitches (anything above a 6:12 slope) require additional fall protection and slow the crew down, which adds labor cost. Complex rooflines with multiple valleys, hips, chimneys, and skylights add flashing work and waste material, since more shingles get cut to fit angles instead of laid flat.

The other major swing factor is what's happening underneath the shingles. If the plywood or OSB decking has rotted from a long-standing leak, or the original roof only has one layer to remove versus two, the scope of work (and the final invoice) changes.

## Shingles or metal: how much does material choice change the price?

Standard three-tab or architectural asphalt shingles are the most affordable roofing material by a wide margin, while standing-seam metal roofing typically costs two to three times more per square installed. The price gap reflects material cost, installation time, and the fasteners and trim work metal requires.

Architectural shingles, also called dimensional shingles, are the standard choice for most North Alabama homes. They carry manufacturer warranties typically in the 25 to 30 year range and handle this region's heat and humidity well. Metal roofing (standing seam or exposed-fastener panel) costs more upfront but can last 40 to 60 years with minimal maintenance, sheds hail impact differently than asphalt, and can qualify for the Alabama [FORTIFIED roof](https://ibhs.org/) designation, a wind-resistant construction standard developed by the Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety that can lower windstorm insurance premiums in Alabama.

## What do tear-off, decking, and permits add to the bill?

A full tear-off, removing the old roofing material down to bare decking, typically adds several hundred to low thousands of dollars to a project depending on how many layers of shingles are being removed and local disposal fees. Decking replacement is priced per sheet and depends on how much rotted or water-damaged plywood the crew finds once the old roof comes off.

Most reroof contracts are written assuming a set number of "bad" decking sheets are included, with additional sheets billed as a change order once uncovered. This is normal and not a sign of a bad contractor; it's impossible to know exactly how much decking is compromised until the old shingles come off. Ask any bidder up front what their included sheet count is and what they charge per sheet beyond that.

Most jurisdictions in Madison County, including unincorporated areas like Harvest, require a building permit for a full roof replacement, though straightforward shingle repairs often don't need one. Confirm permit requirements with your local building department before work starts; a legitimate roofing contractor should pull the permit as part of the job, not leave it to you.

## Will insurance cover a roof replacement after hail or wind damage?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, documented wind or hail damage to a roof, but they do not cover replacement for a roof that's simply old or worn out. The distinction matters because insurers will send an adjuster to confirm the damage was storm-caused before approving a claim.

After a hail or wind event, document the damage with photos before any repairs happen, and keep the adjuster's report. Depreciation also plays a role: a policy paying actual cash value (ACV) reimburses based on the roof's age and condition, while a replacement cost value (RCV) policy pays the full replacement price minus your deductible. ## Repair or replace: how do you know which one you need?

A roof needs full replacement rather than a repair when the damage is widespread, the shingles are past their expected service life, or the decking underneath has failed in multiple spots. A handful of missing shingles or a single flashing leak is usually a repair; granule loss across the whole field, curling or cupping shingles, or leaks in more than one room point toward replacement.

Other signs it's time to replace: the shingles are at or near the manufacturer's rated life (commonly 20 to 30 years for architectural asphalt), you can see daylight through the attic decking, or a hailstorm left bruising across most of the roof surface rather than isolated spots. If you're unsure which category your roof falls into, a visual inspection settles it faster than guessing from the ground.
