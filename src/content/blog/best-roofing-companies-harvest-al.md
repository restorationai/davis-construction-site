## What should be in a roofing estimate before you sign anything?

A complete roofing estimate names the exact shingle or metal product line, the number of squares, the underlayment type, and the full payment schedule, not just a bottom-line total. If a quote is a single number with no breakdown, that's a sign to ask more questions before signing.

At minimum, a written scope should spell out:

- Tear-off versus overlay, and what happens if rotten decking is found underneath
- The specific shingle or metal panel brand and product line, not just "architectural shingles"
- Underlayment type and ice-and-water shield placement at valleys and eaves
- Per-sheet pricing for any plywood decking replacement discovered mid-job
- Disposal and cleanup, including magnetic nail sweep of the yard
- A payment schedule tied to milestones, not full payment up front
- A realistic start window and estimated completion time

## How does a workmanship warranty differ from a manufacturer's warranty?

A manufacturer's warranty covers defects in the shingles or metal panels themselves, often 25 to 50 years depending on the product line, while a workmanship warranty covers the installer's labor, flashing, and fastening pattern, and is usually much shorter, often 2 to 10 years. The product brand only protects you from bad materials; the workmanship warranty is what protects you from a bad install.

Ask every contractor for their workmanship warranty length in writing, and whether it transfers if you sell the house within that window. Some manufacturers also offer extended or enhanced warranty tiers that require the installer to be enrolled in a specific certification program, so it's worth asking directly which program, if any, a contractor participates in rather than assuming. ## What should you watch out for when hiring a roofer in Harvest?

Be cautious of anyone who shows up door-to-door after a storm, pressures you to sign the same day, or asks for full payment before work starts. A legitimate local roofer has a permanent business address, a phone number that gets answered during normal business hours, and references from jobs completed nearby, not just a truck with magnetic signage.

Storm-chasing crews that pull a permit and move on to the next town are a real problem after hail events, and they're often the hardest to reach if a flashing detail fails two years later.
