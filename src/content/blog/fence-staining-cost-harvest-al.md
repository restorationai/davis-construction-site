## How much does fence staining cost in Harvest, AL?

Most homeowners in Harvest pay $2 to $5 per linear foot to have a wood fence stained professionally, with the total driven by fence height, whether both sides get coated, and the stain product used.

A typical 6-foot privacy fence running 150 linear feet (a common lot-line length in Harvest's newer subdivisions) usually runs $450 to $900 for one side only, and $700 to $1,400 if both sides get stained. Staining both sides matters more than most homeowners expect: a fence that only gets coated on the street-facing side still absorbs moisture from the backyard side and will show uneven weathering within a season or two.

The price swings on three things: prep work (a heavily mildewed or gray fence needs more pressure washing and sometimes a brightener treatment before stain will take evenly), the stain grade (solid-color stains cost more per gallon than semi-transparent or clear sealers but last longer), and access (a fence buried under mature landscaping takes longer to mask and coat than an open fence line). ## How soon can you stain a new pressure-treated fence?

Most new pressure-treated pine fences need two to six months to dry out before stain will absorb and bond properly, not the few weeks many homeowners expect.

Pressure-treated lumber is infused with preservative under high pressure, which leaves the wood saturated when it's installed. Stain applied to wet wood sits on the surface instead of soaking in, and it peels within a year. The standard test: sprinkle water on the fence. If it beads up, the wood is still too wet. If it soaks in within a few minutes, the fence is ready. Drying time depends heavily on season and whether the fence sits in sun or shade, which is why the range is wide. Wood moisture content and weathering behavior are well documented by the USDA's [Forest Products Laboratory](https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/), the federal research arm for wood science.

Once the fence is dry enough, prep is straightforward: pressure wash to remove mill glaze, dirt, and any mildew, then let it dry another 24 to 48 hours before applying stain. Skipping the wash step is the most common reason a fresh coat of stain fails early.

## Oil-based or water-based stain, which holds up better in North Alabama?

Oil-based stain penetrates deeper and tends to look richer on fresh pine, while water-based (acrylic) stain dries faster and resists mildew better in humid climates, which matters more here than the finish color.

Oil-based products soak into the wood grain and are easier to recoat later without stripping, but they cure slower in high humidity, which can stretch dry time during a muggy Alabama summer. Water-based stains dry faster and clean up easier, and most modern formulas hold up well against the mildew growth that humid, shaded fence lines are prone to around Madison County. Neither option should be applied in direct, high-heat sun. The surface dries too fast and leaves visible lap marks. Early morning or late afternoon application in the 50 to 90 degree range gives the most even result.

If the fence is vinyl instead of wood, none of this applies, since vinyl never needs stain or sealer. ## How often do you need to restain a fence in Harvest's climate?

Most wood fences in North Alabama need restaining every two to five years, with the shorter end of that range applying to clear sealers and the longer end to solid-color, pigmented stains.

Semi-transparent stains typically need a fresh coat every two to three years here because UV exposure breaks down the pigment faster than it would in a drier climate. Solid-color stains carry more pigment and hold up three to five years. South- and west-facing fence runs, which take the brunt of Alabama's afternoon sun, will fade noticeably sooner than shaded sections. Staining is different from sealing: stain adds color and UV protection, while a clear sealer mainly blocks moisture without changing the wood's appearance much. Many homeowners choose a stain-sealer combination product so one coat does both jobs.

One more thing worth checking before you pick a color: several Harvest subdivisions carry HOA covenants that specify approved exterior stain colors for fences, especially on lots backing up to common areas. It's worth a quick look at your covenant before committing to a solid color.
