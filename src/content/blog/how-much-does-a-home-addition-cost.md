**TL;DR:** A home addition in North Alabama runs roughly $120-$250 per square foot finished, depending on foundation type, whether you're adding a bathroom, how the new space ties into your existing roof and HVAC, and your finish level. A 400 sq ft master suite addition lands between $55,000 and $90,000 for most Madison and Huntsville homeowners. A simple 200 sq ft bedroom addition can come in under $35,000. A 1,000 sq ft in-law suite with a full bath and its own HVAC zone can push $200,000 or more. These are planning ranges, not quotes.

If you're sitting on a house that's too small and you're trying to figure out whether an addition pencils out before you call a contractor, the number you need first is total finished cost, not cost per square foot. Cost per square foot is a useful shorthand, but it hides the scope. Two 400 sq ft additions can differ by $40,000 based on whether one needs a full bathroom, a new foundation footing, and a rerouted HVAC trunk, while the other just extends an existing slab and adds a bedroom. This post breaks down what actually drives the number in North Alabama, with planning ranges by finished size.

## How Much Does a Home Addition Cost by Square Footage?

For North Alabama, expect finished addition costs in these planning ranges:

| Finished Size | Low End | High End | Typical Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 sq ft | $24,000 | $40,000 | Single bedroom, no bath, slab or crawl space tie-in |
| 400 sq ft | $55,000 | $90,000 | Master suite with bath, or two-room addition |
| 600 sq ft | $80,000 | $140,000 | Large suite, in-law space, or family room + bath |
| 1,000 sq ft | $130,000 | $220,000 | Full in-law suite, bonus room over garage, or large multi-room addition |

Add 10-15% to whatever number you land on for contingency. Additions almost always surface surprises once walls open up: undersized electrical panels, HVAC systems at capacity, footings that need reinforcement, or framing that wasn't built to current IRC span requirements. Budget the contingency before you start, not after.

These ranges assume a fully finished addition with drywall, flooring, trim, and HVAC. An unfinished shell (framed, dried-in, but no interior finishes) runs roughly 40-50% less, but most homeowners adding livable space want it finished.

## What Actually Drives the Cost of a Home Addition?

The per-square-foot number is the output. These are the inputs that determine where you land in the range.

**Foundation type and soil conditions.** This is often the biggest variable homeowners don't anticipate. North Alabama has a mix of slab-on-grade homes (common in 2000s-2020s Madison subdivisions) and older crawl space homes (common in 1970s-80s ranch stock throughout Madison and Huntsville's South side). Tying a new addition into an existing slab is relatively straightforward. Adding a new crawl space or pier-and-beam foundation requires excavation, footings, block or poured walls, and a vapor barrier. In areas with expansive clay soils, which are common across the Tennessee Valley, a geotechnical assessment may be required before the footing design is finalized. Budget $8,000-$20,000 for foundation work alone on a mid-size addition, more if the site has drainage complications.

**Roof tie-in.** The point where the new roof meets the existing structure is one of the most labor-intensive parts of any addition. Cutting into an existing roof, matching the pitch, tying in the ridge or valley, and flashing the intersection correctly takes skilled carpentry. If your existing roof is near the end of its life, contractors will often recommend replacing the whole roof at the same time rather than tying new materials into aging shingles. That adds cost, but it avoids a leak problem two years later. Expect $6,000-$15,000 for roof tie-in work on a typical addition, depending on complexity.

**HVAC extension or new zone.** Your existing HVAC system may or may not have capacity to serve the new square footage. A load calculation (Manual J) will tell you. If the system has headroom, extending ductwork into the addition is the lower-cost path, typically $3,000-$7,000. If the system is already sized close to its limit, which is common in 1980s homes that have had previous additions, you may need a mini-split system for the new space. A single-zone mini-split runs $3,500-$6,500 installed. That's actually not a bad outcome: the new space gets its own thermostat, and you're not stressing the existing equipment.

**Plumbing for a bathroom.** Adding a full bathroom to an addition is the single line item most likely to move you from the low end of the range to the high end. Rough plumbing for a new bath, including supply lines, drain lines, and a vent stack, runs $5,000-$12,000 before fixtures. If the addition is on a slab and the existing drain lines run in the opposite direction from the new bath location, expect the higher end of that range. A half bath (toilet and sink only) is considerably cheaper: $3,000-$6,000 rough-in. A full bath with a tiled shower adds another $4,000-$10,000 in finish work depending on tile selection and fixture grade.

**Finish level.** Builder-grade finishes (standard vinyl plank flooring, basic trim package, stock cabinets if applicable, mid-grade fixtures) keep you in the lower half of the range. Mid-grade finishes (LVP or hardwood flooring, solid wood trim, tile shower, semi-custom cabinetry) push you toward the middle. High-end finishes (custom tile work, hardwood throughout, designer fixtures, coffered ceilings) push you to the top. The framing, foundation, and mechanical work cost roughly the same regardless of finish level. The finish level determines the last 20-30% of the budget.

**Permit and engineering fees.** Madison and Huntsville both require building permits for additions. Permit fees in Madison typically run $500-$1,500 depending on project value. If the addition requires a structural engineer's stamp (common for second-story additions or anything that modifies load-bearing walls), add $1,500-$3,500 for engineering. These are not optional costs. Unpermitted additions create problems at resale and can void homeowner's insurance coverage for work done without permits. For more on what the permitting process looks like for a larger remodel, see our post on [how long a home remodel takes](/blog/how-long-does-a-home-remodel-take/).

## What Does a 400 Sq Ft Master Suite Addition Cost?

A 400 sq ft master suite addition is the most common addition scope in North Alabama, and it's a useful benchmark. Here's how the budget typically breaks down for a mid-grade finish level in Madison or Huntsville:

- **Foundation (crawl space or slab extension):** $10,000-$18,000
- **Framing, sheathing, and roof tie-in:** $18,000-$28,000
- **Windows and exterior doors:** $4,000-$8,000
- **Siding to match existing:** $3,000-$6,000
- **HVAC extension or mini-split:** $4,000-$7,000
- **Electrical (outlets, lighting, panel capacity check):** $4,000-$7,000
- **Plumbing for full bath:** $7,000-$12,000
- **Insulation and drywall:** $5,000-$9,000
- **Flooring, trim, paint:** $6,000-$10,000
- **Bath tile, fixtures, vanity:** $5,000-$12,000
- **Permits and engineering:** $1,500-$3,000
- **Subtotal:** $67,500-$120,000
- **10-15% contingency:** $6,750-$18,000
- **Total planning range:** $74,000-$138,000

The wide range reflects the difference between a straightforward slab-on-grade tie-in with builder-grade finishes and a crawl space foundation with custom tile work and a designer fixture package. Most mid-grade master suite additions in this area land between $80,000 and $100,000 all-in.

## Do I Need a General Contractor for a Home Addition?

Yes, for almost every addition. A home addition involves structural work, foundation work, roofing, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and finish trades, all of which need to be sequenced correctly and inspected at the right stages. Managing that coordination yourself while also working a full-time job is a significant undertaking, and mistakes in sequencing (like closing up walls before the rough-in inspection) can require costly tear-out.

A licensed general contractor handles permitting, subcontractor scheduling, inspection coordination, and scope management. Their fee is typically built into the project cost as overhead and profit, not a separate line item. For a detailed breakdown of how general contractor fees work, see our post on [how much a general contractor costs in Alabama](/blog/how-much-does-a-general-contractor-cost/).

For additions that connect to existing living space, the contractor also needs to understand how the new structure ties into the existing one: load paths, header sizing over new openings, shear wall requirements. This is structural work that requires experience and, in many cases, an engineer's review.

## How Do I Get an Accurate Estimate for My Addition?

A reliable estimate requires a site visit, a set of drawings (even preliminary), and a clear scope. Here's what to have ready before you call contractors:

1. **Know your square footage target.** Even a rough footprint (20x20, 20x30) gives a contractor enough to ballpark foundation and framing costs.
2. **Know what rooms you need.** Bedroom only? Bedroom plus bath? Full suite with walk-in closet? Each adds cost.
3. **Know your finish level preference.** Bring photos of kitchens or baths you like. It saves a lot of back-and-forth.
4. **Ask about the permit process upfront.** A contractor who doesn't mention permits in the first conversation is a flag.
5. **Get at least two written bids.** Bids that come back thousands of dollars apart usually differ in scope, not just price. Ask each contractor to walk you through what's included.
