**TL;DR:** In Madison, AL, a cosmetic bathroom refresh (new vanity, fixtures, paint) typically runs $3,500 to $8,000. A full remodel that keeps the existing layout runs $12,000 to $22,000. A primary bathroom remodel with a tiled walk-in shower and a layout change can reach $28,000 to $50,000 or more. The single biggest cost driver is whether plumbing and drain lines move, which also triggers a City of Madison plumbing permit.

If you're staring at three remodeling quotes for the same hall bath in your Blue Springs or Bradford Creek home and one number is nearly triple another, the spread almost always comes down to one decision: are you keeping the shower, toilet, and vanity in the same footprint, or are you moving plumbing lines to change the layout. Everything else, tile grade, vanity style, fixture brand, matters, but it moves the price by a few thousand dollars. Moving plumbing moves it by tens of thousands.

## How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Madison, AL?

Most Madison-area bathroom remodels fall into five cost tiers, and knowing which one you're actually asking for is the fastest way to stop comparing apples to oranges across bids.

| Scope | What's included | Typical cost range |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic refresh | New vanity, toilet, faucet, paint, hardware, lighting; same layout | $3,500 - $8,000 |
| Tub-to-shower or shower replacement | Remove tub, install new shower base or pan, surround or tile, glass enclosure; drain and supply stay put | $6,000 - $12,000 |
| Full remodel, same layout | New tile floor, tub or shower, vanity, toilet, lighting, exhaust fan; fixtures stay in their current spots | $12,000 - $22,000 |
| Primary bath with tiled walk-in shower | Larger shower footprint, double vanity, tiled low-curb or curbless shower, upgraded fixtures | $20,000 - $35,000 |
| Full remodel with a layout change | Moving the toilet, shower, or vanity; new drain lines, new supply lines, possible subfloor repair | $28,000 - $50,000+ |

On a per-square-foot basis, bathroom remodels in this market typically land between $150 and $300 per square foot for same-layout work, climbing past $350 per square foot once you're relocating plumbing or expanding the footprint. A small 5x8 hall bath and a 12x14 primary bath can carry a similar price per square foot, but the primary bath's total bill is naturally much higher because there's more of it.

## What's the single biggest factor that changes the price?

Moving plumbing is the biggest lever in any bathroom remodel budget, often adding $8,000 to $20,000 or more over a same-layout job. Relocating a toilet, shower drain, or vanity means cutting into the slab or subfloor, running new drain, waste, and vent (DWV) lines, and re-routing supply lines, work that a same-layout remodel never touches.

Homes on a slab foundation, common across newer Bradford Creek and Redstone Village subdivisions, require breaking and repouring concrete to relocate a drain, which adds both cost and a few days of schedule. Homes with a crawl space, more typical of the older ranch stock near the Research Park Boulevard corridor, are usually cheaper to re-plumb because the work happens from underneath instead of through the slab.

If your goal is a bigger shower or a double vanity but your current layout already has the space, keeping fixtures in their existing locations can cut thousands off the project without sacrificing much of the upgrade you actually want. The [NKBA](https://www.nkba.org/) publishes planning clearances for bathrooms (minimum space in front of a vanity or toilet, for example) that are worth reviewing before you decide a layout change is necessary.

## What permits does a bathroom remodel need in Madison?

Madison requires a plumbing permit any time fixtures are added or moved, per the City of Madison Building Department. A straight fixture swap, same vanity, same toilet, same shower location, generally doesn't trigger a plumbing permit, but an electrical permit is typically required for new circuits, GFCI outlets, or added lighting.

Full details on what triggers a permit, inspection scheduling, and required documentation are on the city's [permitting page](https://www.madisonal.gov/640/Permitting). Pulling permits matters beyond avoiding a fine: an unpermitted plumbing or electrical change can complicate a home sale or insurance claim down the road, since inspectors and appraisers check permit history on remodeled bathrooms.

If your home was built before 1978, which describes a good share of the ranch homes in Madison's older sections, federal EPA rules (the Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule) require work practices any time a remodel disturbs painted surfaces. Ask your contractor how they handle this before demo starts; it's a real regulatory requirement, not an upsell. More detail is available directly from the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead).

## What hidden costs should I budget for?

Budget 10 to 15 percent above your contracted price as a contingency for what gets found once the old shower surround or tub comes out. In homes 20 years or older, it's common to find subfloor rot, mold around the drain, or a cracked shower pan that was never visible until demo.

A few specific things that show up often in Madison's housing mix:

- Galvanized supply lines behind original 1970s-80s ranch bathrooms, which a contractor will usually recommend replacing with PEX while the wall is already open
- Subfloor rot around the toilet flange or tub, from years of slow leaks nobody noticed
- Undersized bathroom exhaust fans that don't meet current ventilation needs, especially relevant given North Alabama's summer humidity
- Cast iron or older PVC drain lines that need a coupling replacement when a new fixture ties in

None of these are guaranteed, but all of them are common enough that a contractor who quotes a bathroom remodel with zero contingency built in is either underbidding or hasn't opened enough old walls to know better. ## How do I get a reliable estimate?

A reliable bathroom remodel estimate starts with an in-person walkthrough, not a phone quote, because the contractor needs to see your current plumbing, framing, and fixture layout before committing to a number. Ask for a written scope that separates labor, materials, and allowances for tile or fixtures, so you can see exactly where your money is going.

Many homeowners finance a full bathroom remodel through a home equity line of credit or a personal loan rather than paying cash up front; your lender or a financial advisor can walk you through which option fits your situation. On the contractor side, ask whether the quote already accounts for a layout change or assumes fixtures stay in place, since that single assumption is usually the difference between the low bid and the high bid on the same project.
